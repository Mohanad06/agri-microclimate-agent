# UC Davis Extension: Tomato Growing & Heat Stress Guide
Source: University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources (UC ANR Publication 8159)
URL: https://anrcatalog.ucanr.edu/pdf/8159.pdf
Crop: Tomato
Category: Temperature, Germination, Heat Stress, Irrigation

## Germination and Planting Thresholds
Soil temperature plays a critical role in tomato seed germination and transplant success.
- Minimum soil temperature for germination: 10°C (50°F). Germination below this threshold is extremely slow and susceptible to soil-borne pathogens.
- Optimum soil temperature range: 15°C to 30°C (60°F to 86°F). In this range, seedling emergence occurs within 6 to 10 days.
- Maximum soil temperature for germination: 35°C (95°F). Germination drops significantly above this limit.
- Transplanting recommendation: Transplants should be established in the field only after the danger of frost has passed and soil temperature at a depth of 5 cm (2 inches) reaches at least 15°C (60°F).

## Flowering and Fruit Set Temperature Limits
Tomatoes are highly sensitive to extreme temperatures during the flowering and fruit set stages.
- Daytime temperature threshold: Temperatures consistently exceeding 32°C (90°F) during flowering lead to reduced pollen viability and blossom drop.
- Nighttime temperature threshold: Night temperatures above 21°C (70°F) or below 13°C (55°F) impair proper fertilization, resulting in poor fruit set, "catfacing" deformities, or flower abortion.
- Extreme heat: Continuous heat exposure above 35°C (95°F) for more than 4 consecutive hours significantly increases the risk of complete flower loss.

## Irrigation and Moisture Requirements
Tomato plants require consistent soil moisture to prevent physiological disorders.
- Water stress indicators: Inadequate watering combined with high heat index values triggers blossom-end rot, a physiological disorder caused by calcium deficiency in the fruit.
- Soil wetness proxy: Maintaining root-zone soil wetness (GWETROOT) above 0.4 on a normalized scale is optimal for vegetative and fruit development. Deficits below 0.25 trigger vegetative wilting and stress.
